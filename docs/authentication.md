# Authentication architecture (prepared, disabled)

Email verification establishes control of a mailbox. It does not establish that someone is currently a student or employee. Eligibility must require both a verified address and a current, non-revoked organisation membership supported by reviewed evidence. There is no login, registration, verification-email, session or eligibility endpoint in Phase 1; all future privileged actions must fail closed until the full flow is implemented.

Use a standards-compliant OpenID Connect provider (not selected). Prefer institution or employer federation and verified organisation claims where available. Backend verifies issuer, audience, signature, expiry, nonce and PKCE code flow; only configured issuers are trusted. The stable identity key is (issuer, subject). Account linking requires reauthentication and explicit confirmation, never automatic email matching. Require provider-confirmed email verification or a backend-generated, hashed, single-use, short-lived email challenge. Rate limit challenges and use generic responses to avoid account enumeration.

Do not use `.edu`, `.ac.uk`, `.org` or similar suffixes to decide eligibility. A reviewed organisation-domain registry supports real schools and employers on any TLD. Review must establish institutional existence and control (for example federated metadata and independently reviewed DNS/domain ownership evidence), record a review reference, and support revocation. Parse an actual email address, normalise its domain with IDNA, reject malformed/ambiguous input, and match exact reviewed ASCII domains only. Subdomains need explicit review; never use `endswith`. Domain ownership alone is not proof of current employment/student status; shared providers, alumni mailboxes, contractors and multi-tenant domains require additional membership evidence or manual review.

Organisation memberships record evidence, verification time, expiry and revocation. Domain registry uniqueness assumes a domain belongs to one organisation; shared domains must use federation or manual membership verification instead. Reverification and provider/domain revocation must invalidate eligibility promptly. Do not persist identity documents or raw proofs without a reviewed retention and access policy; evidence_reference is an opaque audit reference. Reviewer authentication, authorisation and audit events require design before activation.

Future browser sessions should use opaque server-managed sessions in HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite cookies, with CSRF protection on mutations and restricted credentialed origins. Avoid browser localStorage tokens. FastAPI must enforce authorisation independently of the Next.js UI. Distinguish authentication, eligibility, project ownership and reviewer privileges. Provider credentials and callback URLs will be supplied only after provider selection; no fake credentials are configured.
